## 00. FIRST — DID I UNDERSTAND YOU?

Played back, in the order it arrived:

1. **eBay holds ~90% of estate pipes globally** — for buying lots and for selling.
   It is the venue by default, not by merit.
2. **eBay treats pipes as nothing.** No category department, no attribution
   requirements, no condition vocabulary, no one to appeal to.
3. **High fees, forced promotions, and suspension without cause.** This happened
   to Faridunhill. A working business disappeared overnight.
4. **Etsy was the escape** — started from scratch, and Farid was among the few who
   built the pipe category there.
5. **The proposal: a page or branch on faridunhill.com** that becomes the venue
   the category deserves. Auction and buy-now. Enforced standards — no listing
   without a brand, 8+ photos, dimensions, a real condition vocabulary, and a
   description built from the photographs rather than casual prose.
6. **The larger frame:** gather the whole pipe business into one firm — the Hunter
   buys lots, restoration works them, three channels sell them, the Encyclopedia
   and ID system and archive make Faridunhill the reference for data and prices.
7. **The goal underneath all of it:** be accepted by the community as at least an
   important leg. Not tolerated — trusted.

**Yes. And the last item is the real objective. Everything else is a method.**

That reordering is the point of this file. The venue was proposed as the solution.
It is one possible expression of the objective, and probably not the first one.

## 01. WHY eBAY WILL NEVER FIX THIS

Worth stating precisely, because the strength of the whole idea rests on whether
this weakness is temporary or permanent.

**It is permanent, and the reason is business model, not neglect.**

eBay's model is maximum listings at minimum friction. That is the entire machine.
A form that *demands* brand attribution, chamber diameter, eight photographs and a
condition vocabulary is the opposite model — it is friction deliberately added to
raise quality and lower volume.

eBay cannot build that for pipes without contradicting the thing that makes eBay
work at scale. **So this is not a gap that closes when someone at eBay finally
notices the category. It stays open.** [I]

**Second permanent weakness: eBay has no concept of a persistent object.** It has
listings that expire. The same pipe sold four times over twenty years produces
four unconnected listings and then nothing. There is no object. There is no
history. There is nowhere for a record to live.

**That second weakness is the larger one, and it is the one this file is built
on.**

**Third: the suspension.** Tobacco-adjacent policy on eBay is arbitrary and every
seller in the trade knows it. This is not only Faridunhill's grievance — it is a
population of sellers who have either been removed or are quietly afraid of it.
That population is the cold-start list, and it is motivated by something stronger
than lower fees.

## 02. THE FORK — AND IT MUST BE CHOSEN, NOT STRADDLED

This is the hardest section and it recurs in every file in this portfolio. A2 §06
raised it for the index. A3 §08 raised it for pricing. It is not a detail that
keeps reappearing. **It is one unresolved fork that everything else is downstream
of.**

> **A dealer cannot also be the neutral ground.**

- **Chrono24** became the world's watch venue and **has never sold a watch.**
- **Discogs** became the dominant marketplace for used records and **has never
  sold a record.**
- **eBay** does not sell goods.
- **PWCC** ran auctions, held inventory, and indexed the market — and was
  investigated for shill bidding and removed from eBay. [S/U]

**The pattern is unbroken: the gatherers were neutral, and the ones that were not
got attacked on exactly that ground.**

Faridunhill is the largest dealer here. A collector community owned by the largest
dealer is, to a collector, a place to be sold to. It does not matter whether that
is true. It matters that it is the first thing anyone will think.

**The only known resolution is structural separation that is real and provable:**

- The reference layer holds **no inventory** and links out to **all** sellers,
  including direct competitors.
- The Faridunhill store is **a seller on it**, treated identically.
- **The ranking rule is published**, and Faridunhill's own listings receive no
  privilege under it.
- Never buy some of a man's pipes and consign the rest in the same transaction.

Separation is expensive to prove and cheap to lose. **But the alternative is not
separation-lite. The alternative is choosing to be a very good dealer and
abandoning the umbrella.** Both are respectable. Doing half of each is the version
that fails.

## 03. HOW COMMUNITIES IN THIS SHAPE ACTUALLY GATHERED

Farid's question — *what is the best way to gather all collectors in one place
under our umbrella* — has an answer, and it is not the one the venue idea assumes.

**Nobody has ever gathered a collector community with a marketplace. They gathered
around information, and the marketplace arrived later.** [I, but the cases below
are consistent]

| Operator | Started as | Marketplace came | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discogs** | a community-built **database of releases** | years later | data → venue |
| **Chrono24** | a **listing directory** for existing dealers | later | index → venue |
| **PCGS** | **grading** | registry after | standard → registry |
| **Card Ladder** | **data** | acquired by the venue | data → exit |
| **NEPIP** (pipes) | a registry, built first | never | **died** |

**NEPIP is the local warning and A2b already diagnosed it: no write-path. A
registry nobody has a reason to update dies.**

### Discogs is the closest analogue and deserves attention [S/U — verify]

Small obsessive base. Objects that are used, condition-dependent, and unique in
condition if not in type. An incumbent (eBay) with far more traffic.

What it did, in order:

1. **Catalogued every release** — free, useful even if you never bought anything.
2. **Published a condition vocabulary** — Mint / Near Mint / VG+ / VG / Good /
   Fair / Poor — which spread across the whole category, **including to eBay
   sellers who had never used Discogs.**
3. **Made every release a permanent page.**
4. **Then let listings attach to those pages.**

It beat eBay inside its category without ever having more traffic than eBay,
because it owned the *object*, and eBay only owned the *transaction*.

**Farid's proposed condition codes — new / mint / excellent / very good / good /
fair — are step 2 of that sequence. Step 1 is missing, and step 1 is the one that
makes step 4 possible.**

## 04. THE MECHANIC — THE LISTING ATTACHES TO THE OBJECT

This is the single most important idea in the file.

**On eBay, a listing floats free and then vanishes.** Nothing accumulates.

**In the model proposed here, the pipe has a permanent page** — registration
number, Grain Print, attributes, logbook, essay, and every recorded sale of it and
of pipes like it. **Listings attach to that page. The page outlives them all.**

Consequences worth stating one at a time:

- **A seller lists here for a reason unrelated to traffic.** He is not renting an
  audience. He is minting a permanent record that raises the pipe's resale value
  forever — including when it resells on eBay to a buyer who has never heard of
  Faridunhill. That is the aircraft-logbook effect from A2b, and it is a reason to
  use venue #2 that survives venue #2 having less traffic than venue #1.
- **The cold-start problem inverts.** Normally supply waits for demand. Here supply
  has an independent motive.
- **Discussion becomes searchable.** Q&A attached to a specific pipe is something
  no forum can do — forum threads are chronological and effectively unfindable.
  6,147 members and 848,693 messages at Pipe Smokers Den is a category whose
  knowledge is real and almost entirely unretrievable. [H — from A1]
- **It can point at eBay.** In the earliest stage the "where to buy" link goes
  wherever the pipe actually is. This costs nothing, requires no payments
  infrastructure, and makes Faridunhill the layer *above* the venues instead of a
  competitor to them.

> **Do not replace eBay. Supersede it.** Let eBay keep the transaction and take
> identity, standard, history and price away from it. One man cannot out-liquidity
> eBay. One man with 8,000 records can out-*remember* it.

## 05. THE LADDER

Each stage funds and feeds the next. The failure mode of the whole portfolio is
nine things running in parallel at 60%, all competing for one pair of hands.

**Stage 0 — REFERENCE.** Object pages, the attribute standard, the index, the
Encyclopedia, free identification. No inventory. No payments. **Cannot be
suspended by anyone.** This is the only stage with zero platform risk, and it is
the stage the community is actually asked to gather around.

**Stage 1 — OWN STORE.** faridunhill.com, buy-now, own inventory. Every item sold
mints a permanent record. Proves the standard on objects Farid controls.

**Stage 2 — CONSIGNMENT.** Other people's pipes, physically in hand. Merchant of
record, one Stripe account, no payment facilitation. **Physical possession is the
only condition under which the standard is enforceable** — you cannot make a
stranger take eight honest photographs.

**Stage 3 — DROPS.** Announced time, everything live at once, fixed price, first
come. Urgency without competitive bidding.

**Stage 4 — LISTING ATTACHMENT.** Third-party listings attach to object pages;
the transaction happens on the seller's own venue. Faridunhill touches no money.
**This is Chrono24's original model and it delivers "the platform" without the
wall in §09.**

**Stage 5 — AUCTION / FULL MARKETPLACE.** Only if Stage 4 demonstrates bidding
density.

### Why auction is last and not first

The serious collector base is **15,000–40,000 worldwide** [H — A1]. An auction
with four bidders realises less than eBay with thirty — and **a failed auction is
public.** It is a permanent visible statement that nobody wanted the pipe. It
damages the seller, the object and the venue simultaneously.

An unsold fixed-price listing is just still for sale tomorrow.

**Auction is the format that punishes thin liquidity hardest. It is the reward for
having gathered the community, not the tool for gathering it.**

## 06. CONSIGNMENT — THE TWO OBJECTIONS, ANSWERED

Farid raised both and they are the correct two.

### "The seller values his pipe higher than it is worth."

**Never give a valuation. Valuations are arguments.** Give a **range with the
comps behind it**: *estimate $280–340; here are the last eleven recorded sales of
this brand × era × shape.*

The disagreement then runs between the consignor and the record, not between the
consignor and Farid. **Every other dealer in the category says "trust me, it's
worth $300." Faridunhill can say "look."** That archive is the only reason this
conversation can be non-adversarial, and no competitor can hold it.

### "He will find out what I sold it for, and that spoils the relationship."

**Inverted. Disclosure is the product.** Sotheby's and Christie's publish every
hammer price; the consignor always knows. Resentment does not come from the seller
knowing the price — it comes from him **finding out later** and realising he was
not told. **Opacity is what poisons it, not transparency.**

So the strict policy Farid wants is the opposite of secrecy: fixed commission
stated upfront · reserve set by the consignor **in writing before photography** ·
estimate backed by published comps · sale price visible the day it sells ·
payment on a stated schedule. Nothing negotiated in the moment.

### The move that ends the argument before it starts

**Two doors.** Cash now at a lower number, Faridunhill carries the risk — or
consignment at a higher expected number, the consignor carries the time. Both
priced off the same comps.

**A man who chooses his own terms cannot resent them.**

## 07. PRICING — SUMMARY ONLY, FULL TREATMENT IN A3

The archive is the reference layer's foundation and **a nominal sold-price archive
is a decaying asset that looks like an appreciating one.** Every year it gains
records and every record becomes a worse guide to the present.

Published raw, it becomes a machine for suppressing prices in this category —
including Faridunhill's own.

Rule: **store nominal price + date, never edited. Compute the indexed range at
display time, never stored.** The archive then re-prices itself forever with no
manual work.

Observed anchor: Farid sells the Dunhill at **$259–325** where CPI implies ~$231
and gold implies ~$528. **His hands have been running an index at roughly CPI plus
a quarter for years.** [I — two data points, one brand; see A3 §09.1]

**Gold's role: a published line, never the asking price.** Global and unrevised —
which matters for buyers in Germany, Japan and the Gulf where CPI means nothing —
but too start-date dependent to be a standard on its own.

**Full argument, the objections, and the reviewer attacks: A3 — THE DEFLATOR.**

## 08. THE WRITE PATHS — WHAT KILLED NEPIP

A registry nobody but the owner updates is dead on arrival. Three mechanisms that
grow the record layer **without requiring collectors to cooperate**:

1. **The repair bench.** Every pipe that arrives for stem work passes through
   Farid's hands, gets photographed, Grain Printed, logged, and goes home with
   papers it did not have. **No permission required — just the pipe.**
2. **Free identification.** A collection enters the market when a man dies and his
   daughter opens a cabinet of forty pipes she cannot read. Her current options
   are an antique dealer at $150 for the lot, an estate sale at $3 each, or a
   mislabelled eBay listing. This is the point of maximum information asymmetry in
   the whole business and nearly everyone in the trade exploits it. **Being the
   one who does not is worth more than the margin from exploiting it once.**
3. **The gap costs money.** From A2b: an undocumented period is a hole in the
   object's price, so owners have a financial reason to keep logging. This is the
   mechanism NEPIP lacked.

**And the unserved need nobody in this category has touched:** every serious
collector past sixty-five has the same quiet worry — what happens to the cabinet.
His wife does not know. His children do not want them. Documenting a living
collector's cabinet gives him something he actually wants and makes Faridunhill
the named destination for a collection that may not come to market for fifteen
years. **That is how you stop competing for supply.**

## 09. WHAT COULD KILL THIS

- **Payments.** ⚠️ **The wall.** Faridunhill's own store on Stripe is one
  conversation. A marketplace holding and disbursing third-party sellers' money on
  a tobacco-adjacent domain is a materially different underwriting review, and
  **may simply not be approvable.** This must be answered before anything is
  designed around Stage 5. Stages 0–4 are deliberately constructed to not require
  it.
- **The fork, unresolved.** §02. Player and referee. This is the recurring
  unresolved item across A2, A3 and this file.
- **Nine projects, one pair of hands.** Hunter, restoration, three channels,
  Encyclopedia, ID system, registry, consignment, repair, Monster. Vertical
  integration works when the stages run **in sequence**, each funding the next. It
  fails when they run in parallel and all sit at 60% forever.
- **The eye is the moat and the bottleneck.** Every part of this depends on
  Farid's attribution judgment. **The ID system is the only mechanism by which that
  eye exists outside his head — which may mean it is not one of nine projects but
  the one the other eight are waiting on.**
- **Etsy.** Faridunhill has already lost one platform. Etsy permits an
  independent site; it does not permit soliciting buyers off-platform. **Do not
  lose the second venue while building the third.**
- **The books.** Facts from the reference library are free. Reproduced text and
  scanned pages are not. A public reference built on lifted content is one
  complaint from deletion.
- **The demographic bet.** A1 says the collector base is dying: supply rises now,
  the bid thins later. Consolidating a shrinking category is a real strategy — but
  it is a bet on **owning the infrastructure everyone still buying must pass
  through**, not a bet on prices rising. **Turn fast. Do not hold inventory hoping
  it appreciates.**

## 10. FOR THE REVIEWERS — READ BEFORE RESPONDING

The known failure mode of this council is agreement: reviewers restating the
document in more confident language, scoring it highly, and adding nothing.

**A review that concludes this is strong has not done the work.** Find the soft
spots. Six are named below and there are certainly more.

1. **§03 — the data-before-venue pattern.** Five cases, selected after the
   conclusion was formed. **Name the counter-examples: marketplaces that gathered
   a collector community with no prior information layer.** If there are many, §03
   collapses and the venue-first idea was right all along.
2. **§04 — the attachment mechanic.** Does a seller really list somewhere with less
   traffic in exchange for a permanent record? **This assumes sellers think about
   an object's resale value in ten years. Most do not.** Is there evidence of any
   seller anywhere behaving this way?
3. **§02 — the fork.** Is separation actually provable by a one-man operation, or
   is it a lawyer's fiction that fools nobody in a 15,000-person market where
   everyone knows everyone? **If it is not provable, say so — the correct answer
   may be to abandon the umbrella and be an excellent dealer.**
4. **§05 — the ladder.** Six stages is a decade of work. **Is this sequencing, or
   is it a way of never having to choose?** Which stages could be cut entirely
   without losing the objective?
5. **§08 — free identification.** Attractive and unpriced. **How many hours per
   week does it consume before it returns anything, and what happens when it
   scales past one man?** A free service that cannot be turned off is a liability.
6. **The unasked question.** This entire file assumes gathering the community is
   achievable and valuable. **What if the 15,000–40,000 collectors do not want an
   umbrella — from anyone — and the honest answer is that this category supports a
   very good dealer and nothing more?** A friendly reviewer will not raise this.

**Do not score it. Do not summarise it back. Answer the six.**
