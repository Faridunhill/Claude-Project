## ★ The one error found — `325` is not a gold fineness
C23 reports a hallmarked gold band carrying the leopard's head, **"325 (9 carat gold)"** and
FC. Checked today: British hallmarking law recognises six finenesses — 999, 990, 916 (22ct),
750 (18ct), 585 (14ct) and **375 (9ct)**. **There is no 325.** Nine carat gold is 375.
Sources: [Assay Office London — UK hallmarks](https://www.assayofficelondon.co.uk/hallmarking/uk-hallmarks) ·
[The Complete Guide to British Hallmarks](https://www.whatisthismark.com/guides/complete-guide-to-british-hallmarks) ·
[What does a 375 gold stamp mean](https://isle-of-wight-jewellery.co.uk/blogs/news/what-does-a-375-stamp-mean-on-gold-jewellery)

**This is not an invented fact** — it is attributed to the page, and he added his own caution
that "the 1969 attribution is the page's, not mine." So check 10 is **not** capped. But it is
a wrong number that reached our file unchallenged, and a pipe expert reading a hallmark
should know 375 on sight. Either the source mistyped and he passed it through, or he
mistyped. Either way the correction goes in the cabinet and the source gets a note.

**Why this matters beyond one digit:** hallmark date letters are the only *exact-year* dating
method available for banded pipes. He identified that himself in his gaps list. A cabinet
that carries a wrong fineness next to a hallmark rule will fail in front of exactly the
collector we are trying to win.
