## 1. IT WORKS, AND MUSEUMS ALREADY DO IT

This is not an experiment. X-radiography of **wooden objects** is standard conservation
practice, and what it reveals is exactly what a pipe collector wants to know:

> internal cracks · **previous restorations and concealed repairs** · internal supports
> and pins · insect damage · grain structure · material layering

The Field Museum, the Penn Museum and the Smithsonian's Asian Art museum all run
X-radiography on collection objects for precisely this. A published example: an x-ray of
a wooden Bodhisattva revealed **three hidden wood screws** from an old restoration.
Medieval wooden sculptures have been CT-imaged to document internal iron supports and
insect damage.

**A briar pipe is a small wooden object with a drilled interior.** It is an easier
subject than a life-size sculpture.

*The citation matters as much as the technique: "we use the same imaging method museums
use on wooden sculpture" is a sentence a level-3 collector respects immediately.*

## 2. WHAT AN X-RAY PROVES — AND WHAT IT MUST NEVER CLAIM

| It proves | It cannot prove |
|---|---|
| **Hidden repairs** — pins, dowels, glue lines, filled cracks, metal inserts | **Who made it.** No X-ray identifies a maker |
| **Originality of the stem/tenon** — re-drilled bores, replacement tenons, sleeves | **When it was made.** X-ray does not date anything |
| **Drilling quality** — is the draft hole centred, does it meet the chamber at the heel | Grade, value, or "genuineness" as a brand |
| **Remaining wall thickness** — burnout risk, over-reaming | |
| **Internal cracks** invisible from outside | |

**Law for this track: an X-ray is a CONDITION and ORIGINALITY instrument, never an
authentication or dating instrument.** That is not a limitation — it is the exact
mandate the buyer-message data already handed us: authenticity was asked in **1 message
out of 1,053**; what collectors actually want to know is *condition* and *what parts are
original*. This answers that question with physical evidence.

**And it must be said plainly to Farid before he spends a dollar:** an honest X-ray
programme will sometimes kill a sale by revealing a hidden repair he would otherwise
have sold in good faith. Under LAW 1 that is the point, not a bug. It is also precisely
what converts a level-3 professional into a public advocate.

### The bonus nobody has noticed
**Every pipe's drilling is slightly unique, and an X-ray is not scrapeable.** Surface
photos can be copied from a listing by anyone; an interior image can only be made by
holding the object. That makes the X-ray an **instance fingerprint of the interior** —
the Indentation Layer (addendum H2.1) applied where no competitor can follow.

## 3. HOW TO ACTUALLY GET IT — THREE ROUTES, CHEAPEST FIRST

### Route A — Pay per image. **Start here.**
- **A veterinary clinic with digital radiography** is the cheapest and fastest door. A
  pipe is a trivial subject for equipment that images cats daily; there is no patient,
  no anaesthesia, no risk. Call a few small-animal practices and ask what they charge
  for a couple of radiographs of an inanimate object.
- **A dental office with CBCT** (cone-beam CT) gives a small-volume 3D scan — a true
  cross-section at any plane, from an intact pipe.
- **An NDT lab.** In/near New Jersey the search surfaced **Integrity Test Labs (Newark,
  NJ)** for radiographic testing, and **MISTRAS Group** as a major RT provider.
  **NDT.org keeps a New Jersey company directory.** Caveat: industrial NDT firms are
  built around welds, castings and pipelines — a pipe will be an unusual request, and
  their minimums are set for industrial jobs.
- **A CT service bureau** (e.g. Jesse Garant Metrology, 4NSI, Micron) sells by the hour,
  per scan, or per project — highest quality, highest cost. Feasibility review first.
- **A university lab.** Rutgers / NJIT / Princeton-class materials and imaging
  facilities often sell scan time and are used to odd samples.

**Do this on 5–10 premium pipes before considering any purchase.** The test answers the
only questions that matter: are the images compelling, do collectors react, and what
does one image actually cost?

### Route B — Own a self-shielded cabinet system *(if volume justifies it)*
The correct ownership class for a workshop is a **cabinet system** — the shielding is
the box, and it is designed for non-medical facilities. Refurbished and demo units
exist (Glenbrook Technologies sells refurbished systems such as the JewelBox line;
Matsusada and Manncorp sell benchtop units; Surplus Record lists used machines).
**Prices are not published — quote required.** Expect this to be a serious capital
decision, not a gadget.

### Route C — **Benchtop CT: no.**
Benchtop X-ray CT scanners run **$150K to well over $500K**, and even a 100 kV or lower
configuration is **$150K–$225K**. Rent it, never buy it.

### The cheap handheld dental unit — read this before buying one
Portable/handheld dental X-ray units with a digital sensor are sold online for roughly
**$600–1,000**. That is real, and it is the "cheap machine" the question was reaching
for. **But it is not a shortcut**, for three reasons:
1. It is an unshielded, hand-held ionising-radiation source designed for a trained
   operator using it on a patient for a fraction of a second, in a room set up for it.
2. It is still a registrable machine under NJ law (§4) — the price of the box is not the
   price of compliance.
3. Intraoral sensors are small; a pipe needs a larger field, so image quality for a
   whole pipe will be poor without a plate scanner.

**Recommendation: do not start here.** Start with Route A, where somebody else already
owns the machine, the shielding, the training and the licence.

## 4. THE CONSTRAINT THAT DECIDES IT: NEW JERSEY REGULATES THE MACHINE, NOT THE USE

Owning an X-ray machine in New Jersey is a **registered, inspected activity** under the
NJDEP **Radiation Protection Element, Bureau of X-Ray Compliance**:

- A **"Radiation Producing Machine Registration Form"** must be filed with the Bureau
  **within 30 days of installation.**
- **Every** X-ray installation requires a **radiation safety survey performed by an
  appropriately qualified individual.**
- Registration is **per X-ray tube** — two tubes on one control panel means two
  registrations.
- Industrial facilities pay a **$40 application fee per tube** plus the prorated annual
  renewal, invoiced by the Department.
- Selling, relocating, storing or disposing of a unit must be reported **in writing
  within 30 days.**

**Read the fee schedule correctly: $40 is the application, not the cost of compliance.**
The survey, the qualified surveyor, the shielding, the operator training and the
recurring renewals are the real budget — and they are the reason Route A exists.

*Verify all of this directly with NJDEP before purchasing anything. Regulations change;
this file is a snapshot of a web search, not legal advice.*

## 5. HOW THIS FITS THE RULE WE ALREADY WROTE

Farid's own instinct closed the loop: *"one 3D anatomy model — more logic and under
control."* Correct, and the two are not competing:

| Asset | Class | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **The 3D anatomy model** | **KNOWLEDGE** | Teaches how any pipe works — infinite sections, angles, animated airflow. Built once, used in every episode |
| **The X-ray of a specific pipe** | **EVIDENCE** | Proves what is inside *this* pipe. Belongs to that pipe's listing, dossier and archive record forever |

**The model teaches. The X-ray proves.** Same rule as §6.3b of the character brief, now
applied to a camera instead of a pencil.

## 6. THE DECISION POINTS (Farid's)

1. **Run the Route-A test?** 5–10 premium pipes at a vet clinic or dental CBCT, and one
   phone call to Integrity Test Labs for comparison. Cheap, reversible, and it produces
   real images to judge.
2. **If the images are good — how far does the badge go?** Every premium pipe, or only
   the museum tier? The per-image cost from step 1 decides this, not enthusiasm.
3. **Commission the 3D anatomy model?** Independent of everything above, and it is the
   asset the daily repair episodes keep returning to.
4. **Publish the flaws?** The programme only earns trust if a bad result is published as
   readily as a clean one. This is the honesty law, priced in advance.
