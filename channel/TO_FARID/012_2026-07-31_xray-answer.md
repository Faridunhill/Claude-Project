# 012 — X-ray: it works, museums already do it, and the cheap machine is a trap

Farid, 2026-07-31: *"first time I hear about it, but it gives me very good marketing and
quality ideas… is there any cheap machine like a CT scan or X-ray? If so I will do it for
all premium pipes to prove honest and go up in the collector level ladder."*

Researched, sourced, and written up in `docs/XRAY_EVIDENCE_TRACK.md`. Short version:

## 1. It is not an experiment — it is standard museum practice
X-radiography of **wooden objects** is routine conservation work at the Field Museum,
the Penn Museum and the Smithsonian. What it reveals is exactly what a collector wants:
internal cracks, **concealed old repairs**, hidden pins and supports, insect damage. One
published case: an X-ray of a wooden Bodhisattva revealed **three hidden screws** from a
past restoration.

**A briar pipe is a small wooden object with a drilled interior — an easier subject than
a sculpture.** And the sentence *"we use the same imaging method museums use on wooden
sculpture"* is one a level-3 professional respects on sight.

## 2. What it proves — and the line we must never cross
**Proves:** hidden repairs · replaced or re-drilled tenons and stems · drilling quality
(is the draft hole centred, does it meet the chamber at the heel) · remaining wall
thickness / burnout risk · internal cracks.
**Cannot prove:** who made it, or when. **An X-ray never dates and never authenticates.**

That limit is a gift, not a problem: your own buyer data said authenticity was asked in
**1 message out of 1,053** — what collectors actually want is **condition and what is
original**. This answers that with physical evidence.

**Say yes with your eyes open:** an honest X-ray will sometimes kill a sale by showing a
hidden repair you would have sold in good faith. Under LAW 1 that is the point — and it
is exactly what turns a professional into a public advocate for us.

**And a bonus nobody has noticed:** every pipe's drilling is slightly unique, and an
interior image **cannot be scraped from a listing** — it can only be made by holding the
object. That makes the X-ray an *internal fingerprint*, the Indentation Layer applied
where no competitor can follow.

## 3. The cheap machine — the honest answer
- **Benchtop CT: no.** $150K to over $500K; even a low-kV configuration is $150K–225K.
- **Cabinet X-ray system** (the shielding IS the box — the right class to own): used and
  refurbished units exist (Glenbrook, Matsusada, Manncorp, Surplus Record). **Prices not
  published — quote required.** A capital decision, not a gadget.
- **Handheld dental unit, ~$600–1,000 online.** Real, and it is what your question was
  reaching for — **but do not start here.** It is an unshielded hand-held radiation
  source meant for a trained operator in a prepared room, it is still a registrable
  machine under NJ law, and an intraoral sensor is too small to image a whole pipe well.

## 4. ★ The constraint that actually decides it — New Jersey registers the machine
Under **NJDEP, Bureau of X-Ray Compliance**: registration form filed **within 30 days of
installation** · a **radiation safety survey by a qualified individual for every
installation** · registration **per X-ray tube** · **$40 application fee** per tube plus
annual renewal for industrial facilities · written notice within 30 days if you sell,
move, store or dispose of it.

**Read that correctly: $40 is the paperwork, not the compliance.** The survey, the
surveyor, the shielding, the training and the renewals are the real budget. Verify with
NJDEP directly before buying anything — this is a web-search snapshot, not legal advice.

## 5. So: rent before you own
- **A vet clinic with digital radiography** is the cheapest, fastest door. A pipe is a
  trivial subject for a machine that images cats all day. Call a few small-animal
  practices and ask the price of two radiographs of an inanimate object.
- **A dental office with CBCT** gives a small 3D volume — a true section at any plane,
  from an intact pipe.
- **NDT labs near you:** **Integrity Test Labs, Newark NJ** does radiographic testing;
  **MISTRAS** is a major RT provider; **NDT.org** keeps a New Jersey directory. Warning:
  industrial NDT is built for welds and castings, so expect industrial minimums.
- **CT service bureaus** (Jesse Garant, 4NSI, Micron) sell per scan or per project.
- **A university lab** (Rutgers / NJIT / Princeton class) often sells scan time.

**Do 5–10 premium pipes this way first.** That test answers everything: are the images
compelling, do collectors react, and what does one image really cost? The answer decides
whether the badge covers every premium pipe or only the museum tier.
