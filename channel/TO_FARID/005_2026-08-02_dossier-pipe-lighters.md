## 1. THE MARKET — who the brands are, and what they cost

| Brand | What it is | Asking prices seen |
|---|---|---|
| **IM Corona "Old Boy"** | The classic pipe lighter. Japanese. Angled flame for the bowl. Decades in production. | **$160–$256** at a specialist dealer; also sold via Amazon and most pipe tobacconists |
| **Xikar Pipeline** | Purpose-built for pipes — 45° tilting soft flame, fuel window, tamper + scraper built in, lifetime warranty | **~$50** |
| **Zippo (pipe insert)** | The cheap, universal entry point | low tier |
| **Lotus / Visol / Vector** | Mid-tier soft-flame competitors | mid tier |

**A disputed fact, carried with both sources.** One buyer's-guide article puts the Old Boy at
*"around $20"*; a specialist dealer lists IM Corona at **$160–$256**. Those cannot both be right.
Most likely the article confused the Old Boy with a budget copy. **I have not resolved it, so I
am not claiming a price.** Your own first purchase will settle it — that is the plank-by-plank
replacement.

## 2. THE WEAK POINT — what buyers complain about

This is the quality-gap method: read what buyers say sellers do badly.

**Xikar** — despite the lifetime warranty, the complaints are consistent and they are about
*reliability*:
- butane leaking (one buyer's leaked before leaving the shop; the replacement wouldn't strike)
- won't stay lit — the spark fires before the fuel arrives
- flint wear, and burners clogging into a weak flame
- a whole thread on a **pipe** forum titled *"Xikar lighters… junk"*

**IM Corona Old Boy** — the opposite reputation: *"reliable, sturdily built, beautiful."* Its one
recurring complaint is flint contact with the striker needing adjustment.

**So the gap is not price. It is CONFIDENCE.** The cheap end works badly and the good end costs
$160+ and still has a fiddly flint. Between those sits a buyer who wants to be told: which butane
grade, how to change a flint, why it leaks, what to do when it won't strike.
