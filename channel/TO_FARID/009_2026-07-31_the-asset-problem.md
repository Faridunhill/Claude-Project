# 009 — "Everything on screen we need to invent it" — no, we don't

Farid, 2026-07-31: *"don't think the character is the only problem — every episode
needs the pipe with anatomy, all the pipe details for the maintenance, plus tools,
everything on screen we need to invent it."*

You are right that it is the bigger half. But the answer is a law we already have.

## THE LAW: characters are DRAWN. Objects are PHOTOGRAPHED. Never the reverse.

**We must not draw the pipes — not because it is hard, because it is dishonest.**

A drawn Dunhill stamp is an **invented** stamp. A drawn date code is an invented date
code. A drawn hallmark or nomenclature line is fabricated evidence, published by a
project whose entire product is *"every fact traces to a source."* And the level-3
collector — the exact man whose endorsement we are hunting — is the one person alive
guaranteed to spot it.

So we never invent them. **We already own them:** 2,000+ photographs, 264 catalogued
items, the ark. **The pipes are the one thing in this whole show we never have to
create.**

And this is an upgrade, not a compromise. A collector does not want a *drawing* of rim
burn, oxidation, or a stamp under raking light. He wants the real one. Ours.

## What that produces: THE EVIDENCE CUT

Every episode alternates two registers:
- **The story** — Professor, Assistant, her mistake, his correction → drawn ink panels.
- **The evidence** — the actual pipe, the actual stamp, the actual damage → **our own
  photographs, captioned with the source.**

The photo appears deliberately *as* a photograph — full-bleed with its citation, or
pinned on the workbench inside the drawn scene. **The format is the honesty law made
visual.** Drawn art carries the story and never a fact.

This also deletes the anatomy worry from the illustrator's job: when precision matters,
we cut to the real thing.

## The math: the library is FIXED, not per-episode

Animation calls it a prop bible — draw once, reuse forever:

- **8** generic pipe shapes (billiard, bent, apple, dublin, pot, freehand, meerschaum,
  churchwarden), 3 views each — generic only; a *specific* pipe is always a photograph
- **1–3** labelled anatomy cutaways (rim, chamber, shank, mortise, tenon, draft hole,
  button, band) — the most reused asset in the series, and an encyclopedia illustration
  in its own right
- **~20** tools — reamer, tamper, cleaners, micromesh, buffing wheel, retort, wax, files
- **~30** process-step panels (ream · clean shank · de-oxidise · sand · buff · wax ·
  polish), hands included, recombined across dozens of episodes
- **~6** workshop backgrounds, 2–3 angles
- **1** character design pass

**≈70–90 drawn assets, commissioned ONCE.** Episode 1 is expensive. Episode 5 reuses
most of it. Episode 30 is nearly free. **That falling curve is what "engine" means** —
and it only works because we moved the facts out of the drawings and into the photos.

If the library ever binds (an angle nobody drew), the scale-up is one toon-shaded 3D
model per generic shape and tool — one model, every angle, forever, and photogrammetry
from our own photos is the natural route. **Not now.** After the library proves itself.

## What this changes in the commission

The designer job is **not** "two characters." It is characters **+ the prop bible +
the workshop set + the written shape/colour rules** that let asset #91 be added later
without a visible style seam. Scope it that way in the first conversation — adding it
afterwards costs more and looks worse.

**One line to put in the contract in bold:** *no maker's marks, stamps, hallmarks, date
codes or brand nomenclature are ever to be drawn. Where a panel needs one, leave it
blank for a photograph.*
