## Your ruling is accepted and it reorders the budget
**The defect/repair library is built first.** It is the daily engine. Sawing drops to
last place. Five routes to the cross-section, cheapest and best first:

1. **The layered vector master** *(do this — already inside the design commission)*.
   Anatomy is KNOWLEDGE, so it may be drawn. One cutaway, drawn **over a photo of a
   real pipe** so proportions stay honest, with toggleable layers: airflow · chamber
   and wall thickness · mortise/tenon fit · **filter variants** (balsa, 9 mm charcoal,
   9 mm meerschaum, adapter, screw system) · cake stages · burnout · bent version.
   Zero saws. One asset, dozens of episodes, any zoom.
2. **X-ray a real intact pipe.** A vet clinic or industrial NDT shop will do it for a
   small fee, and nothing is cut. It shows the **real drilled airway inside a whole
   pipe** — draft-hole alignment, mortise depth, whether the drilling is centred.
   **Nobody in this hobby publishes that.** A CT scan goes further: one scan, sections
   at any plane, plus a 3D model. That is an episode by itself: *"we X-rayed three
   pipes to see who drills straight."*
3. **One 3D anatomy model** — infinite sections and angles forever, animated airflow,
   toon-shaded to match the ink. Same budget line as the character commission.
4. **Pipes that arrive already open** — burnouts, split shanks, cracked stems. Free,
   real, no tool, and they come to you on their own. **Standing rule: photograph the
   inside of anything that arrives broken.**
5. **Cutting** — last. And if a dead pipe ever turns up: you do not need a special saw.
   **Sanding one side away** on the disc/belt sander you already own reaches the chamber
   under full control, and vulcanite parts with a razor saw. Not a priority.
