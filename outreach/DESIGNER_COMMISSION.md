## 1. WHO WE ARE AND WHAT WE ARE MAKING

Faridunhill is a collectors' encyclopedia and estate-pipe archive. We are making a series
of short, warm, informative episodes about pipe collecting, dating and restoration,
published free on YouTube and social media.

**We need a designed world: two characters, their workshop, and the props they use.**

The episodes are built as **still illustrated panels with a slow camera move, burned-in
captions and a voice-over.** The characters do **not** lip-sync and do not need to be
animated. This is a design commission, not an animation commission.

## 2. THE STYLE — decided, not open

Inked 2D comic / graphic-novel illustration: **heavy black outlines, flat cel shading,
warm limited palette, subtle paper texture.** Reference: the look of a well-drawn
European comic album, not smooth 3D and not photorealism.

Palette: briar brown, tobacco amber, brass and gold, deep green, warm lamplight. Cool
tones reserved for daylight through a window.

Line weight: heavy on the outer silhouette, lighter inside — the characters must read at
thumbnail size.

## 3. THE CHARACTERS

### The Professor
- **Based on photographs of a real man** (supplied). He must be **recognisable to people
  who know him** — but as a *designed character*, not a filtered photograph. Deliberate
  exaggeration is wanted.
- **Please decide which three or four features carry his identity** (we believe: the brow
  line, the nose profile, the jaw, the shape of the glasses) — exaggerate those and
  simplify everything else away. **Do not transcribe skin detail, pores, or the exact map
  of his moles.** At most two facial marks, redrawn at character scale.
- **Character:** elegant, slim. A master of pipes, restoration and repair. A scholar, not
  a salesman — he closes a book and names it at the end of every episode. Patient, dry
  humour, quietly proud of his workshop.
- **Costume, fixed and non-negotiable:** small Irish flat cap · glasses · warm brown
  waistcoat / workshop dress · **a pipe in his hand that is NEVER lit — no smoke, no
  flame, no glow, in any frame, ever.**
- **Defining expression: warmth with authority.** If his neutral face reads tired or sad,
  the design has not succeeded.

### The Assistant
- **Invented. She is nobody's likeness**, so you are free — the only requirement is that
  she is reproducible.
- Warm, charming, a beginner's enthusiasm. She makes the honest mistakes every new pipe
  smoker makes and the Professor corrects her; **her mistake is each episode's subject.**
- **Arc across seasons:** employee → falls in love with the craft → becomes genuinely
  skilled → eventually leads her own episodes. The design should age gracefully with
  that: same character, growing confidence in posture and dress.
- She reads as a colleague and a learner. **Not a mascot, not decoration.**
- **Costume signature:** one colour that is hers alone (we suggest deep teal or plum, so
  she never dissolves into the workshop browns), with a work apron over it as she grows.

## 4. THE PROP BIBLE — this is most of the job

Episodes are assembled by recombining a fixed library. Please design it as a **system**,
not as illustrations for one episode.

| Group | Quantity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic pipe shapes** | 8, three views each | billiard · bent · apple · dublin · pot · freehand · meerschaum · churchwarden. **Generic only** — see §6 |
| **Anatomy cutaway** | 1 master + a bent variant | Layered so parts can be shown or hidden: airway · chamber & wall thickness · mortise/tenon fit (**a good fit and a poor one**) · **air passage drilled correctly vs drilled too high** · filter variants (balsa, 9 mm charcoal, 9 mm meerschaum, adapter, screw system) · cake build-up stages · burnout |
| **Stem & bit types** | 1 comparison plate | saddle · tapered · combination · military/army · screw fitting; standard · fishtail · P-lip · denture · wide comfort · double-bore |
| **Tools** | ~20 | reamer · tamper · pipe cleaners · micromesh & sandpaper · buffing wheel · retort · alcohol · wax · files · brushes · stand · pouch |
| **Process panels** | ~30 (3–5 per step), **hands included** | ream · clean the shank · de-oxidise · sand · buff · wax · polish. Hands are how this show teaches — they are not optional |
| **Workshop backgrounds** | ~6 plates, 2–3 angles each | the bench · the shelf of finished pipes · the drawer of "patients" (broken pipes) · the lamp corner · a window · a wide establishing view |

## 5. DELIVERABLES

1. **Turnarounds** — front, ¾, profile, back, both characters.
2. **Expression sheets** — at least 8 each: warm · amused · stern · curious ·
   disappointed · delighted · explaining · listening.
3. **Hand poses** — holding a pipe, tamping, pointing, presenting an object.
4. **Costume sheet**, including the cap, glasses and unlit pipe.
5. **Colour script / palette** with hex values.
6. **Silhouette sheet** — both characters as flat black shapes.
7. **The prop bible** of §4.
8. **Written shape and colour rules** — what may change between shots, what may never
   change, and how a future artist adds prop #91 without breaking the world.
9. **Files:** layered source (PSD/AI/Procreate/Krita — your choice) **plus** flat PNGs
   with transparency at high resolution.

### One structural requirement, please read it
Every asset is delivered **as a separate transparent PNG**, named to a simple id
(`prof-explaining-3q`, `tool-reamer`, `bg-bench-a`). Our system composites layers by id.
**Please do not deliver finished composite scenes** — a scene we cannot take apart is a
scene we cannot reuse, and reuse is the entire economics of this series.

## 6. TWO RULES THAT ARE ABSOLUTE

1. **Never draw a maker's mark.** No stamps, hallmarks, date codes, brand nomenclature or
   logos of real pipe houses — not even invented ones, not even as illegible squiggles.
   Where a panel needs one, **leave the area blank**; we drop in a photograph of the real
   object. Our entire project rests on facts being traceable to a source, and a drawn
   stamp is a fabricated fact.
2. **The pipe is never lit.** No smoke, no flame, no ember, in any frame.

## 7. HOW WE WILL JUDGE THE WORK *(shared in advance so nothing is a surprise)*

- **Silhouette test** — filled solid black, is the character still identifiable?
- **Thumbnail test** — at 64 px, is it still clearly him?
- **The same-person test** — five separate panels side by side: could a stranger cast
  them as one character in one show? *This is the test that matters most.*
- **The child test** — can someone draw the character from memory after ten seconds?
- **The expression test** — covering the costume, does the neutral face read as warm
  authority?
- **The likeness test** — the owner's own judgement: *"is that me?"* Style notes are
  fixable in revision; a lost likeness is not.
