# X-RAY TEST — call sheet and protocol
**For the 5–10 pipe pilot. Authorized 2026-07-31.** Research and sources:
`docs/XRAY_EVIDENCE_TRACK.md`.

**The pilot's job is to answer three questions and nothing else:** are the images
compelling · do they show what we hope (hidden repairs, drilling, wall thickness) · what
does one image actually cost. **Do not buy any equipment until these are answered.**

---

## 1. Who to call, in order

**A. Small-animal veterinary clinics with digital radiography.** Cheapest and fastest.
A pipe is a trivial subject for a machine that images cats all day, and there is no
patient, no anaesthesia, no risk.

**B. Dental offices with CBCT (cone-beam CT).** Gives a small 3D volume, which yields a
section at *any* plane from an intact pipe. Ask specifically for **CBCT**, not a normal
intraoral X-ray.

**C. NDT labs** — Integrity Test Labs (Newark, NJ), MISTRAS, or the NDT.org New Jersey
directory. Expect industrial minimums; a pipe will be an unusual request.

**D. CT service bureaus** (Jesse Garant, 4NSI) and **university imaging labs**
(Rutgers / NJIT / Princeton class) — highest quality, quote per scan or per hour.

## 2. The phone script *(say it plainly; it is a strange request and honesty works best)*

> "Good morning. This is an unusual request and I hope you can help. I restore and sell
> antique smoking pipes, and I would like to X-ray a few of them — the object only, no
> animal and no person. They are wooden, about six inches long, and I want to see the
> drilled air channel inside and whether there are any hidden old repairs. Museums do
> this with wooden objects routinely.
>
> Would you be willing to take two or three radiographs of an object like that, and what
> would you charge? I would bring five to ten pieces in one visit, and I would want
> digital image files I can keep."

**If they hesitate:** *"There is no patient involved at all — it is just the object on
the plate. If it is easier, I am happy to come at a quiet time and pay for the time
rather than the study."*

## 3. What to ask before you go
- **Price per exposure, and price for a visit of 10–20 exposures.**
- **Do I get the digital files, and in what format?** *(Ask for the original DICOM or a
  full-resolution TIFF/PNG. A phone photo of a screen is worthless to us.)*
- **Can we try two or three exposure settings on the first pipe?** Wood is far less dense
  than bone; the first setting will probably be wrong.
- **Can the pipe be laid flat, stem attached, on the plate?**

## 4. What to bring
1. **5–10 pipes chosen to be a real test**, not just the pretty ones:
   - 2 you believe are **completely original**
   - 2 you **know have been repaired** (so we can see whether the repair actually shows)
   - 1 **9 mm filter pipe with the filter seated** — the shot Farid asked for
   - 1 **bent** pipe (the airway curve)
   - 1 **stem alone**
   - 1 **burnout or heavily caked** bowl, if one is to hand
2. **A scale reference in the frame** — a coin or a bank card. Same rule as intake
   photography: an image without scale is an impression, not a measurement.
3. **A numbered list** so every file can be matched back to a pipe. Photograph each pipe
   normally beside its number before it goes on the plate.

## 5. Positions to request (per pipe)
1. **Side-on, whole pipe, stem attached** — the full airway in one line.
2. **Side-on, shank and mortise only, closer** — tenon fit, mortise depth, any pin or
   filler.
3. **Top-down through the bowl** — chamber concentricity and wall thickness.
4. *(filter pipe)* **Shank close-up with the filter seated.**

## 6. What we are looking for — judge the images against this list
- Does the **draft hole meet the chamber at the heel**, or high on the wall?
- Is the drilling **centred** in the shank?
- **Wall thickness** remaining — anywhere thin enough to warn about?
- Any **pin, dowel, screw, sleeve or glue line** invisible from outside?
- Any **internal crack** not visible on the surface?
- Is the **tenon original** or replaced?

## 7. The rules that govern what we publish afterwards
- **An X-ray proves condition and originality. It never dates and never authenticates.**
  No image may be captioned in a way that implies a maker or a year.
- **A bad result is published as readily as a clean one.** If a pipe we are selling shows
  a hidden repair, that goes in the listing. The programme earns nothing if it only
  speaks when the news is good.
- **Every image is stored with the pipe's record** — reference number, date, facility,
  and the settings used, so the image is evidence and not decoration.
- Keep the **originals untouched** forever; annotate copies only.

## 8. After the pilot — the decision
Cost per image decides the scope: every premium pipe, or only the museum tier. Bring the
numbers and the images back and we will write the badge and the listing copy around what
is actually affordable.
