Two estate pipes of the same make and year can differ in value several times over on condition alone. After five thousand sales, this is the grading vocabulary we trust — and the one used in every Faridunhill listing and Pipe Passport assessment.

## The grades

**Unsmoked / New Old Stock (NOS).** No carbon in the chamber, no tooth chatter, original finish intact. Factory-fresh survivors from closed shops. The chamber walls show raw briar, not black cake.

**Excellent.** Smoked but cared for: light, even cake; crisp stamping; stem with minimal oxidation and no deep tooth marks; rim free of charring.

**Very good.** Honest use, professionally cleanable: moderate cake, light rim darkening, surface scratches, mild stem oxidation. Most quality estates arrive here.

**Good / Project.** Heavy cake, rim char, deep tooth marks, dents or fills, worn stamping. Priced accordingly; often worthwhile for rare shapes.

**Damaged.** Cracks, burnout, broken tenons, chunks missing. Sold only as identified spares or restoration challenges, always disclosed.

## What we inspect, in order

1. **Chamber walls** — the deal-breaker. Look for burnout (spongy, charred wood under the cake) and cracks radiating from the rim.
2. **Rim** — charring and "rounding" from careless lighting; can be topped, but topping alters the pipe's height.
3. **Shank and mortise** — cracks at the mortise are structural. Check for old repairs: bands hide breaks.
4. **Stem** — oxidation is cosmetic; tooth-through holes and short tenons are not. Confirm the stem is original to the pipe.
5. **Stamping** — sharp nomenclature adds value; buffed-away stamps subtract it and complicate identification.
6. **Fills and putty** — pink or dead-flat spots in the grain under strong light.

## Restoration: what changes, what doesn't

Professional cleaning — reaming to a thin cake, alcohol treatment of the airway, stem de-oxidation, gentle re-waxing — restores smokeability without erasing history and is standard practice for estate dealers. What we avoid: aggressive buffing that softens stamping and edges, topping without disclosure, and stain jobs that hide fills. **A restored pipe honestly described is worth more than an over-polished mystery.**

## Why grading belongs in identification

Condition is evidence. Original finish confirms era; a replacement stem changes the dating logic; a buffed stamp explains a missing date code. That's why every **[Pipe Passport](/encyclopedia/pipe-passport)** assessment includes condition observations alongside identification — the two questions answer each other.
