Savinelli has been making pipes since 1876, when Achille Savinelli opened his tobacco shop in Milan — and no maker is more systematic about shape numbering. For the estate hunter, the three-digit shape code on the shank is the fastest route from "unknown Italian" to a precise identification.

## The shape number system

Nearly every Savinelli carries a **three-digit shape number** on the underside or side of the shank. The number identifies the shape across all lines — a 320 is the same author shape whether it appears on a budget line or a premium one. Useful patterns:

- The number stays constant across finishes and price tiers, so one reference chart covers the whole catalogue
- A letters suffix (such as "KS" for king size or "EX") indicates an enlarged version of the base shape
- The famous **320 author** and **510 bent** are among the most reproduced shapes — recognizing them by eye is a collector's party trick

## Reading the line names

The line name stamped alongside the shape number places the pipe in the hierarchy:

- **Autograph** — the top of the house: freehand, hand-finished pipes, graded by number
- **Punto Oro** — "gold point," premium smooth pipes marked with a gold dot on the stem
- **Giubileo d'Oro** — golden jubilee line, fine straight-grain finishes
- **Dry System** — Savinelli's engineered condensation system pipes
- Workhorse finishes such as **Roma, Capri, Oscar, Tundra** — honest smokers, made in enormous numbers, superb value on the estate market

## The balsa filter

Savinelli's signature engineering touch: a **6mm balsa wood insert** (later also 9mm) that sits in the tenon and absorbs moisture without a bore-through filter's draw resistance. An estate Savinelli with the stepped tenon that accepts balsa inserts is entirely normal — and the pipe smokes perfectly well with or without the insert fitted.

## Dating a Savinelli

Savinelli did not use explicit date codes, so dating relies on:

1. **Line lifespans** — lines entered and left the catalogue over the decades; the line name brackets the era
2. **Logo evolution** — the shield stamp and stem logo changed in dateable steps
3. **Country wording** — "Made in Italy" conventions and the presence of the shape-number style narrow the window

A Savinelli usually dates to a decade rather than a year — honest precision for an Italian factory pipe.

## What the estate market rewards

Autographs and Punto Oro with clean stamping command the strongest prices; the workhorse lines are the smart buyer's entry into quality Italian briar. Fills are common on lower lines and normal for the grade — judge them against the price tier, not against an Autograph.
