The Comoy family were making wooden pipes in the French Jura in 1825 — before briar itself became the standard — and the firm claims a place among the very first to work briar at all. In 1879 the family brought the business to London, and "Comoy's of London" became one of the defining English factory brands.

## The three-piece C

Comoy's stem logo — the inlaid **"C"** — is famous among collectors for its construction: in the classic era it was a **three-piece inlay** (white ring, blue ring, white center forming the C). The logo's construction is dating evidence:

- **Three-piece inlaid C** — the classic era, the collector's marker
- **Simpler one-piece or drilled C** — later production
- Logo style plus stamping style together bracket a Comoy's within its era

## The lines

- **Blue Riband** — the flagship smooth grade, top-of-catalogue for decades
- **Tradition** — the great standard line, superbly made in huge numbers
- **Grand Slam, Royal, Sandblast lines** and export series — spanning the tiers
- Shape numbers on the shank cross-reference to catalogue charts, as with the other London makers

## Stamping styles as a clock

Comoy's stamping evolved in recognizable steps — the wording, the arrangement of "COMOY'S" over the line name, the "Made in London England" formats. Collectors use these stamping conventions the way Dunhill collectors use date codes: less precise, but consistent enough to place a pipe within a period. Photograph the stamping sharply and comparison does the rest.

## The Cadogan consolidation

Like GBD, Comoy's passed into the **Cadogan** group's consolidated production in the early 1980s. The pre-merger/post-merger divide is the central value question:

- **Pre-merger Comoy's** — independent London factory work; Blue Ribands and early Traditions from this era are the collector's target
- **Cadogan-era** — production shared with sibling brands; honest pipes, thinner collector demand

## Estate market notes

Pre-merger Comoy's, like pre-merger GBD, is quietly undervalued English quality. The Blue Riband in clean condition is a genuinely fine pipe at a modest price; Traditions are the everyman classic. Beware buffed-out stamping — Comoy's identification leans on it more than most brands.
