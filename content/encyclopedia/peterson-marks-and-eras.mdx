Kapp & Peterson has made pipes in Dublin since the 1890s, and while Peterson never used an explicit date-code system like Dunhill's, its **country-of-origin stamps track Irish political history** — which makes them surprisingly dateable.

## The era markers

The wording of the origin stamp is the primary clue:

- **"Made in Ireland" (block, no reference to the Free State)** — spans several early conventions; pre-Free State examples belong to the 1890s–1922 window
- **"Irish Free State"** — 1922 to late 1930s, a tight and collectible window
- **"Made in Eire" / "Eire"** — late 1930s through the 1940s
- **"Made in the Republic of Ireland"** — from 1949 onward; the long modern era

Combined with the style of the "K&P" stamp, hallmarks on silver-mounted pipes, and shape catalogue evidence, these place most Petersons within a decade or better.

## Silver hallmarks: the precise ones

Silver-banded Petersons carry Dublin assay hallmarks — and Irish hallmarks include a **date letter**. On a sterling-mounted Peterson, the hallmark can date the pipe to an exact year, outclassing every other mark on the pipe. Learn the three-mark cluster: Hibernia, the harp crowned, and the date letter.

## The famous systems

**The Peterson System** — the patented reservoir-and-graduated-bore design from the 1890s, stamped "System" with quality grades (Standard, Premier, De Luxe). System pipes with patent-era markings are among the most collectible Petersons.

**The P-Lip.** Peterson's patented mouthpiece, directing smoke upward. Original P-Lip stems in good condition matter to both smoking character and value.

## Notable lines for the estate hunter

- **Sherlock Holmes series** — large-bowled shapes introduced in the late 1980s
- **Kapruf, Shamrock, Killarney** — mid-century workhorse lines, excellent smokers, modest prices
- **Founder's Edition and commemoratives** — dateable to their issue years by definition

## Caveats

- Peterson reused shape numbers across decades — the shape number alone rarely dates a pipe.
- Wartime and transition-year stampings show inconsistencies; treat single-mark conclusions with humility.
- London-made Petersons exist (the company kept a London operation for a period) — "Made in England" on a Peterson is not a fake by default.
