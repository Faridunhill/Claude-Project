## 1. OWNERSHIP AND ERAS

**C1.** The brand was founded in London in 1863 by Frederik Charatan.
Source: *Charatan — Pipes: Logos & Markings*, http://www.pipephil.eu/logos/en/logo-charatan.html — page shows **Updated: Nov 06, 2013**.

**C2.** When his father retired in 1910, Reuben Charatan took over the family business.
Source: same page, same date.

**C3.** All Charatan pipes were handmade until 1973.
Source: same page, same date.

**C4.** The brand name was taken over by Dunhill in 1978.
Source: same page, same date.

**C5.** The brand was sold in 1988 to James B. Russell Inc. (New Jersey, USA).
Source: same page, same date.

**C6.** Between 1988 and 2002 Charatans were crafted by Butz Choquin in St Claude, France.
Source: same page, same date.

**C7.** Dunhill re-purchased the Charatan brand name in 2002, and Colin Fromm (Invicta Briars, Castleford) follows up on freehand production.
Source: same page, same date.

**C8.** The "Family era" is given as the period 1910–1960 (stated beside a Newmarket shape 58).
Source: same page, same date.

**C9.** The "Lane era" is given as the period 1955–1988 (stated beside a Tinder Box Golden Jubilee).
Source: same page, same date.

## 2. ★ DISAGREEMENT ONE — WHEN THE LANE ERA BEGINS

This is the most useful thing I found, because both dates are in circulation and they are **six years apart**.

**C10.** "L in circle denotes a pipe imported into USA by Lane Ltd between **1955** and 1988."
Source: *Charatan — Pipes: Logos & Markings*, http://www.pipephil.eu/logos/en/logo-charatan.html — **Updated: Nov 06, 2013**.

**C11.** "In 1955 Lane took over sole distributorship of Charatan in the US." The Lane Trademark serif and circled L indicates the pipe is from the "Lane Era", given as approximately **1955 to 1979–1984**.
Source: *Dating of Charatans*, https://pipedia.org/wiki/Dating_of_Charatans — **date on page NOT READ: the site answered a bot-verification wall on 2026-09-11 and I did not go around it. Text quoted from the search engine's extract of that page, not from the page itself.**

**C12.** "These were the years during which Herman Lane first acquired F. Charatan & Son (he had been the company's exclusive US distributor since **1955**)" — the acquisition itself is placed at **1961**, and the window 1961–65 is named the **"first Lane era"**.
Source: *Charatan's Make First Lane Era (1961-5) Belvedere Extra Large Free Shape Smooth "Keel" Bent Billiard*, https://www.mbsdpipes.com/product/charatans-make-first-lane-era-1961-5-belvedere-extra-large-free-shape-smooth-keel-bent-billiard-estate-briar-pipe-english-estates — **no date shown on the page**.

**THEY DISAGREE.** pipephil dates the Lane mark from 1955; MBSD separates two events — distribution from 1955, **ownership from 1961** — and reserves "first Lane era" for 1961–65. A circled L therefore does **not** by itself prove ownership by Lane, only import by Lane. Both readings are in the trade. **I do not pick a winner.**

## 3. ★ DISAGREEMENT TWO — WHEN THE LANE ERA ENDS

**C13.** pipephil: the circled L covers imports "between 1955 and **1988**".
Source and date as C10.

**C14.** Pipedia's extract: the Lane era runs to "**1979–1984**".
Source and date-not-read as C11.

**THEY DISAGREE**, and by up to nine years. Note that 1979 is also the year given for Dunhill's control (C17), so the two statements may be describing different things — the end of Lane's ownership versus the end of Lane-marked imports. **Recorded as a disagreement, not resolved.**

## 4. ★ DISAGREEMENT THREE — WHEN DUNHILL TOOK CONTROL

**C15.** "The brandname has been overtoken by Dunhill in **1978**."
Source and date as C10.

**C16.** On the same page, two entries later: "The 'DC' stamping … was introduced when Dunhill took control of the company in **1979**."
Source and date as C10.

**C17.** "The stamp 'Made by Hand' 'in' 'City of London' was used from near the end of 1965 up until Dunhill took control of the company in **1979**."
Source: *Dating of Charatans*, https://pipedia.org/wiki/Dating_of_Charatans — **date on page NOT READ** (bot wall, see C11).

**ONE PAGE CONTRADICTS ITSELF: 1978 in the history paragraph, 1979 in the Belvedere and Lane-Era entries.** A one-year window either way. **Recorded, not resolved.**

## 5. STAMPS THAT CARRY A DATE

**C18.** The "DC" stamping after the shape number means "Double Comfort" saddle bit and was introduced when Dunhill took control. The patent number on the stem applies to that DC bit.
Source: *Charatan — Pipes: Logos & Markings* (Belvedere and Lane Era entries), http://www.pipephil.eu/logos/en/logo-charatan.html — **Updated: Nov 06, 2013**.

**C19.** A registration number seen on a DC saddle stem: **REGD No. 203573**. A different line, Underboar, carries **REG. NO 682372**.
Source: same page, same date.

**C20.** The Coronation grade was introduced between **1965 and 1979**, and "the full script marking (Made by hand in the city of london) was the one used during this period."
Source: same page, same date.

**C21.** Block letters "MADE BY HAND", with other wording in script (for example *City of London* or *Extra Large*), indicates a pipe made **between 1965 and the mid-1970s**.
Source: *Dating of Charatans*, https://pipedia.org/wiki/Dating_of_Charatans — **date on page NOT READ** (bot wall).

**C22.** Block-lettered "MADE BY HAND" on the shank **together with** a Double Comfort mouthpiece indicates a **1961–65** make.
Source: *Charatan's Make First Lane Era (1961-5) Belvedere…*, https://www.mbsdpipes.com/product/charatans-make-first-lane-era-1961-5-belvedere-extra-large-free-shape-smooth-keel-bent-billiard-estate-briar-pipe-english-estates — **no date shown on the page**.

**★ DISAGREEMENT FOUR:** C21 puts block-letter MADE BY HAND at 1965 to the mid-1970s; C22 puts the same block lettering, with a Double Comfort bit, at 1961–65. The two windows barely overlap. **Both recorded.**

**C23.** A hallmarked gold band on one Crown Achievement carried the leopard's head, **325** (9 carat gold) and **FC** (Frederick Charatan); that pipe is dated to **1969**. The Crown Achievement grade sits at the top of about twenty Charatan grades, just below the very rare Suma cum Laude, and the 1970 catalogue price was **1,500 US dollars**.
Source: *Charatan — Pipes: Logos & Markings*, http://www.pipephil.eu/logos/en/logo-charatan.html — **Updated: Nov 06, 2013**. *(This is an English silver-style hallmark on a gold band; the leopard's head is the London assay office mark. The date letter itself is not shown in the quoted text, so the 1969 attribution is the page's, not mine.)*

**C24.** A "CP" logo inlaid in a transparent rondelle is typical of Butz Choquin, so such a pipe is very probably one of those made in France **between 1988 and 2002**.
Source: same page, same date.

**C25.** The Grosvenor example is described as typically French-carved, identified by a slightly different C-and-P linking, "of London" instead of "London England", and a "France" stamp under the stem.
Source: same page, same date.

**C26.** The Underboar line was made from the **1920s to the Second World War**.
Source: same page, same date.

**C27.** "After 1965, some Executive had a carved top to resemble plateau briar."
Source: same page, same date.

**C28.** Charatan's second brand is **Mountbatten**.
Source: same page, same date.

## 6. WHAT THIS IS WORTH TO US

The single most useful line for our cabinet: **a circled L proves import by Lane, not ownership by Lane.** Every source agrees Lane was the US distributor from 1955; only some treat that as the start of the "Lane era". If our cabinet treats a circled L as a 1955 floor it will be right about the mark and wrong about the era, and a collector who knows the 1961 acquisition date will catch it.

The second: **one widely used reference contradicts itself on 1978 versus 1979 for Dunhill.** Any date we publish inside that window must carry both.

## UNVERIFIED

These I believe, or saw in a search extract, but could not open the page and read for myself. They are not evidence until someone opens the source.

**U1.** That the presence or absence of serifs on the CP stamp is a key to dating post-war to 1960-era Charatans, together with the presence or absence of the Lane "L" and of the Double Comfort bit. Seen only in a search-engine extract of *Dating of Charatans* (pipedia.org), which I could not open.

**U2.** That "both the English factory and Lane themselves sometimes, or perhaps even often, forgot to stamp the L on a pipe", and that the factory was known for inconsistent stamping. Same extract, same unopened page. **If true this matters more than anything above, because it means no absence of a mark can ever be used to date a Charatan — which is also our own standing rule.**

**U3.** That from 1863 to 1960 mouthpieces were normal saddle or tapered shapes and that from 1961 the Double Comfort style was used; and that an X in place of the DC after the shape number indicates a saddle bit on pipes made after 1960. Same extract, same unopened page.
